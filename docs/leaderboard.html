What the score does—and does not—say

The official problem description defines a distance-weighted Tversky index with a triangular 300 m kernel, α=0.2 and β=0.8. It scores distance-weighted TP, FP and FN terms. The public score alone does not disclose the number of hidden faults, a recall fraction, or a unique prediction count. Therefore this page intentionally has no “T needed,” recall, ceiling, or reachable/unreachable calculation.

In particular, the earlier live-model calculation used FPw = S − TPw, which is not a general identity: its false-positive term uses prediction-centred nearest-truth credit, while TP is truth-centred. The resulting “recovered truth” and reachability claims are invalid as score estimates; see the metric-identity erratum. No H55/H56 live-model projection is used as a gate.
