What the 0.2778 row establishes — and does not

The official leaderboard associates public best score 0.2778 with participant extradr19 in this snapshot. The owner's prior results list associates 0.2778 with the dotted H33-2-B2 family, but neither the public row nor the owner-maintained registry/live_scores.json is an organizer file-to-hash receipt. The exact local B2 bytes are therefore still unverified as that scored submission. Its spacing/pruning recipe and reported A→B→C ladder are plausible clues about why a sparse family performed well; they do not reveal the private labels, prove generalization, or identify the leaderboard leader's approach.

The documented owner-reported A→B→C ladder (0.2600→0.2708→0.2778) is consistent with a progressively sparse, catalogue-distance-pruned prediction. The organizer states that known catalogue faults are masked; reducing predictions in or near masked regions could reduce prediction mass with little loss of scored true-positive credit, while more generally pruning is beneficial only when removed mass contributes more false-positive penalty than distance-weighted true-positive credit. The exact cells and causal mechanism are not established. The official DTI uses a 300 m distance kernel and weights false-positive and false-negative terms asymmetrically (α=0.2, β=0.8); see the official metric page and organizer's fault-masking clarification. This is a plausible mechanism, not a causal diagnosis: exact file-to-row attribution is unverified and the private final labels are unavailable. The correct scientific answer to “can we beat 0.2778?” is: possible in principle, not demonstrated by this repository. H56B and H56-F are below same-protocol H49 on public-map proxies; those proxies are not the private expert-labelled test set. Improvement requires predictive information that generalizes, and no method can be promised to exceed the leaderboard from current evidence alone.
