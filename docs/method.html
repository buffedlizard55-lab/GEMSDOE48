Method

The metric, the model, and the selection rule

1 · The official metric

Transcribed by two independent sibling repositories from the organizer's problem description (competition 306, page 967) and corroborated independently by the organizers' own reference solution, which trains with TverskyLoss(alpha=0.2, beta=0.8, mode="binary") — the metric's own weights:

k(d)  = max(1 − d / 300 m, 0)
TPw   = Σ_g max_x p(x) · k(d(x, g))
FPw   = Σ_x p(x) · (1 − max_g k(d(x, g)))
FNw   = Σ_g (1 − max_x p(x) · k(d(x, g)))
DTI   = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw)

2 · Caveats on the historical selection arguments

(a) The optimal submission is binary

For one cell of value v that is the argmax of its truth pixel with realised weight k: d/dv[(T₀ + v·k)/(D₀ + 0.2v)] = (k·D₀ − 0.2·T₀)/(D₀ + 0.2v)², whose sign is that of k − 0.2·DTI and is independent of v. Every cell is pushed to 0 or 1, so a graded belief surface is strictly worse than its own binarisation. Asserted numerically in tests/test_h55.py::test_binary_emission_is_dti_optimal.

(b) The 0.2·DTI threshold is conditional

An addition changes both TPw and FPw. Its break-even point depends on its actual incremental FPw and coverage overlap; 0.0556 is the historical surrogate threshold, not a universal rule for a candidate dot.

(c) Adding a pixel can never reduce TPw

TPw is a maximum over emitted pixels. The only risk of an addition is the 0.2 denominator cost, which makes the worst case of an additive candidate exactly computable. This is why H55 is additive-only and why its floor is a bound rather than an estimate.

3 · The removal-regime collapse

Q = Σ_x p(x) max_g k(d(x,g));   FPw = S − Q
FNw = |G| − T;                 T = TPw
DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·S − 0.2·Q + 0.8·|G| + ε)

The historical simplification assumed Q≈T without verifying it against hidden labels. Neither |G| nor T is identified from the owner-reported scores alone.

4 · Identifying |G| from the nested live ladder

A_d2_8 ⊃ B_prune100 ⊃ C_prune200 — verified pixel-wise, not assumed. The removed dots are the catalogue-adjacent ones the organizer masks out of scoring, so all three share one T and |G| is identified. Least squares over the triple gives model |G| = 14,027.5 px, model shared T = 5,212.0. These are conditional outputs, not identified private-label quantities. The three pairwise closed-form solutions are 13,368, 14,089, 15,200 — a ±6 % spread driven entirely by the 4-decimal rounding of the owner-reported scores. The rungs then invert to T = 5,210.4 / 5,216.1 / 5,209.5, agreeing to 0.11 %.

This supersedes the earlier |G| = 12,632 figure in knowledge/research_notes.md, which assumed the removed dots earned exactly the τ = 0.05416 bar rather than zero. Both are recorded; 14,027.5 is the value that makes the three rungs self-consistent.

5 · The sufficient statistic

B_elig = h19-5 backbone AND (distance to public catalogue > 200 m)    (103,805 px)
Cov(X) = Σ_(b ∈ B_elig) max_(x ∈ X) k(d(x, b))
T      = ρ · Cov(X)          ρ = 0.068015   (least squares through the origin)

Fitted against all eight live scores: RMS relative error 1.758 %, maximum 3.628 %. Per-artifact errors: h19_5_backbone +3.63%, d1_5_dotted +0.70%, A_d2_8 -0.98%, B_prune100 -1.18%, C_prune200 -1.81%, h36_rung30 -2.05%, h32_prethin_tip -1.00%, h33d_tip_step -0.48%.

Nine target weightings were tested against the same eight scores — uniform, local backbone density at 7 px and 13 px, its square root and log, radiometric-ratio corroboration, 3 m lidar roughness corroboration, distance-from-catalogue, and density × distance. Uniform wins (RMS 1.758 %, reproduced by scripts/compare_coverage_weightings.py); distance-from-catalogue at 1.659 % and 3 m lidar roughness corroboration at 1.686 % are within 0.1 pp — noise on eight points, and both were specified post hoc; every density weighting is worse (+0.37 to +0.88 pp). A corridor pixel is not more likely to sit near hidden truth because it has more corridor neighbours.

6 · Where the instrument stops

The one out-of-family live artifact — sgmc_offcat_44k, 44,090 px Poisson-disked on SGMC faults > 300 m off-catalogue, owner-reported 0.0512 — inverts to T = 1,026 with Cov = 9,306.8, an implied ρ = 0.11025. The family ρ predicts 0.0316 for a file that scored 0.0512, i.e. the model under-predicts out-of-family T by 38.3 %. The instrument is a within-family tool. Out-of-family mass is priced as a scenario band, never as a prediction — and the bias direction means new signal is more likely under-priced than over-priced.

7 · The selection rule

With the model in hand, the emission problem is a priced maximum-coverage problem:

maximise   ρ · Cov(X) / (0.2·|X| + 0.8·|G|)
over       X ⊇ C ,  X ⊂ (footprint ∧ >200 m off-catalogue ∧ ¬catalogue)

Greedy maximum-coverage selection is used, with exact incremental coverage updates (verified against full recomputation in tests/test_h55.py::test_greedy_incremental_coverage_matches_exact_recomputation). A site is admitted while its marginal coverage gain clears 1.25 × 0.2 × DTI_C / ρ = 0.8169 coverage units. The 1.25 safety factor is pre-registered and makes the choice robust to a ±25 % error in ρ — 14× the instrument's measured error. The unconstrained argmax prefix would admit 2,916 px at live-equivalent 0.2785; the robust prefix admits 1,124.

The total budget is solved from a pre-registered downside rule: if every added pixel earns exactly zero credit, the live-equivalent score must not fall below 0.2778 − 0.0100. That gives n_max = 3,568; 1,776 were used.

8 · Dempster–Shafer, and the honest limit of what it adds

Frame Θ = {F, ¬F}. Discounted simple mass functions m_i(F) = α_i b_i, m_i(¬F) = α_i(1 − b_i), m_i(Θ) = 1 − α_i with α₁ = α₂ = 0.6. Dempster's normalised orthogonal sum gives K = α₁α₂(b₁ + b₂ − 2b₁b₂), Bel(F) = (f₁f₂ + f₁u₂ + u₁f₂)/(1 − K), m₁₂(Θ) = u₁u₂/(1 − K), Pl(F) = Bel + m₁₂(Θ). Where K = 1 the rule is undefined and the code falls back to vacuous mass rather than dividing by zero (Zadeh's 1984 paradox is exactly a consequence of this normalisation; Sentz & Ferson 2002, Sandia SAND2002-0835, document the alternatives — which is why both K and m(Θ) ship).

The limit, stated plainly: because the two parents' coverage differs by 0.3 % while their masses differ by 11 %, and because the metric is binary-optimal, no combination rule can beat the better parent. Dempster–Shafer's real contribution here is structural, not arithmetic: it identifies where two independently-built approaches disagree (K > 0 on 14.70 % of the footprint), and that set is where the gap-closure pool is drawn from and where the conduit anchors are vetoed unless a third source arbitrates. The unassigned mass m(Θ) ships as its own layer exactly as the brief asks.
