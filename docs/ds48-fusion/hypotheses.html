The validation gate these must clear before a slot is spent

A candidate is promotable only if it clears all four:

  1. mass ≤ the 37,654 px of the current live-best artifact;
  2. ≥ 99 % of the live-best artifact's weighted true-positive credit retained on the SGMC off-catalogue layer — the only non-catalogue truth available;
  3. live-anchored safety factor ≥ 2.0 (budget ÷ measured credit destroyed); and
  4. the improvement reproduces in ≥ 3 of 4 spatial blocks.

Rank 1 was validated and it FAILED — not the hypothesis itself, but the first mechanism built on it: the Dempster–Shafer corroboration filter, which deletes the pixels where the two families disagree, spends its budget at safety 0.64 (needs 2.0) at its best operating point and is worse at every other radius. The measurement is in the results table. That is why the hypotheses below, not the filter, are the recommendation for the next session.

1. H48-A · the 200–300 m catalogue-companion band

layersorganizer band det_elev (detrended elevation) and its slope; the published catalogue geometry itself; any DEM-derived curvature
physical signaturea parallel companion trace at 200–300 m from a mapped fault, emitted only where an independent curvature/edge transform is locally maximal — a directional maximum of the second-derivative ridge, not a buffer
why it catches a fault the catalogue lacksThe staff clarification says a new-truth pixel can lie within 300 m of a known trace, and explicitly names “corrections or modifications to existing fault traces”. The live ladder is the evidence: removing the 0–100 m band helped (B = 1), removing the 100–200 m band helped (B = 2), and removing the 200–300 m band hurt — the B = 3 arm spent its budget down to safety 1.27 and won only 3 of 4 blocks. A band that is expensive to delete is a band that holds credit. No mapped fault exists there, which is exactly why it is not already in the catalogue.
how it differs from anything already implementedEverything shipped in the sibling repositories removes mass near the catalogue (B = 1, B = 2, and the flank policies of GEMSDOE27/28/32). Nothing in any repository has ever emitted a thin, physically gated companion band in the 200–300 m annulus.
expected DTI improvementΔDTI ≈ (c − 0.2·DTI)·Δn / D. With D = 18,734 from the live anchor and 4,000 dots at the marginal credit one would need (c ≈ 0.07 to beat the 0.0556 bar), ΔDTI ≈ +0.003. The same arithmetic says c ≈ 0.10 would give ≈ +0.010.
implementation costLow — one annulus, one curvature gate, no new data. Half a session.
data obtainability checkNo external data needed. All layers are organizer bands plus existing_faults.tif.

2. H48-B · Curie-depth lateral gradient from the GeoDAWN magnetic grids

layersGeoDAWN contractor grids 22103_rtp_a1/a2 and 22103_tmi_a1/a2 (official, free, already mirrored locally); cross-checked against geod_2ndinv
physical signaturespectral depth estimation: windowed radial power spectrum of the reduced-to-pole grid → depth to the bottom of magnetic sources (Curie isotherm) → the lateral gradient of that depth surface, a curvature/edge transform in the depth domain, not in the field domain
why it catches a fault the catalogue lacksA Curie-depth step is a subsurface thermal boundary. It marks basement structure under alluvial cover, where no surface trace exists to be mapped into the catalogue. Because the catalogue was built from surface and near-surface mapping, a buried thermal boundary is systematically absent from it.
how it differs from anything already implementedGEMSDOE40's h8-euler-lineament-depthcluster and h45-eulerdepthreadcluster used Euler deconvolution — a single-window structural-index method that returns a source depth per cluster. Spectral depth-to-bottom inversion returns a continuous depth surface whose derivative is the detection statistic. Different estimator, different observable.
expected DTI improvementUnmeasured until built. On the same marginal arithmetic, a field whose marginal credit per dot reaches 0.07–0.10 yields ΔDTI ≈ +0.003 to +0.010 over 4,000–6,000 dots.
implementation costMedium — spectral windows, an inverse problem, and a stability check on the long-wavelength limit. One to two sessions.
data obtainability checkObtainable and already materialised: ScienceBase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ, free and official. Verified present: the file listing in GEMSDOE24/data/external/observed_files.json.

3. H48-C · Frangi vesselness on the radiometric ratio grids

layersGeoDAWN contractor ratio grids ThK, UK, UTh (bands of geodawn_extensions_u8.tif, official)
physical signaturea Hessian-based multiscale vesselness filter (Frangi et al. 1998) applied to the potassium/thorium ratio, tuned to the width of an alteration halo, rather than a threshold on the ratio itself
why it catches a fault the catalogue lacksHydrothermal alteration halos along a fault survive burial and erosion of the fault scarp, so they persist where the surface trace cannot be mapped. A halo is a chemical anomaly; the catalogue is a structural map, so an alteration-only structure is absent from it.
how it differs from anything already implementedGEMSDOE46 fused scarp and radiometric evidence (r11f-scarp-radiometric-fusion, r12-scarp-rad-concordance). A vesselness transform is a ridge detector with an explicit width scale — it finds linear structures at a chosen halo width, which no concordance/fusion arm does.
expected DTI improvementEstimated low-to-moderate: +0.002 to +0.008, because the radiometric grid is 800 m line spacing and only partially covers the footprint.
implementation costLow — one filter, one width sweep, thresholds already in geodawn_rad.json, geodawn_extensions.json.
data obtainability checkObtainable and already materialised (same ScienceBase release, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ).

4. H48-D · χ-anomaly and knickpoint-cluster analysis on the 1 m DEM

layersthe organizer-supplied 1m_DEM_links.csv tile list, mosaicked; USGS 3DEP is the free official source behind it
physical signaturefluvial-network extraction, then (i) a χ (chi) transform of the channel elevation profile and (ii) clustering of knickpoints along straight lines — a topological/network transform, not a local filter
why it catches a fault the catalogue lacksA channel that crosses an active fault develops a knickpoint at the crossing; a row of aligned knickpoints marks a fault whose surface expression is too subdued to map. The catalogue records what a geologist could trace; a chi-anomaly is a drainage-response observable, and it can survive where the trace cannot be seen.
how it differs from anything already implementedThe repository's lidar work used lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif, a 12-band field of local 2 m scarp filters (ex_max, step_max, lapneg_max, downface_max, …). Those are pointwise morphological filters; chi and knickpoint clustering are network-topological and require flow routing, which nothing in the repositories does.
expected DTI improvementUnknown; potentially the largest of the five, because it targets the population the truth set is drawn from — newly identified faults — rather than re-scoring known structure.
implementation costHigh — DEM mosaicking, hydrologic conditioning, flow routing, and profile analysis is at least two to three sessions and needs 10s of GB of tiles.
data obtainability checkThe free official source is USGS 3DEP. Obtainability is NOT claimed from this sandbox: every science-data host tested here returns HTTP 000 (prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com, earthexplorer.usgs.gov, portal.opentopography.org, earthquake.usgs.gov). A ready-to-run fetcher is the correct deliverable, and this idea must not be proposed as viable until the tiles are actually in data/.

5. H48-E · strike-coherent skeleton of the upward-continued TMI grid

layersTMI_up150 (contractor total magnetic intensity upward-continued to 150 m, a band of geodawn_extensions_u8.tif) and the organizer tc band (band 6, “a magnetic field derivative for edge detection”)
physical signaturethe maximum of the horizontal gradient magnitude of the continued field, skeletonised and then gated on strike coherence with the regional stress field (geod_shearrate, geod_2ndinv)
why it catches a fault the catalogue lacksUpward continuation suppresses shallow sources and passes the deeper basement edges that control fluid pathways. A deep, strike-coherent edge with no surface trace is not in a surface catalogue.
how it differs from anything already implementedGEMSDOE32 measured tc transforms and FALSIFIED its rank-1 tilt-angle candidate (10 variants, 0 of 4 blocks beaten), and its own general law is “transform only the layers that are raw fields; the organizers have already differentiated the derivative products”. This hypothesis deliberately breaks that law on a third-party grid that the organizers did not differentiate, and adds a stress-coherence gate that no prior arm used.
expected DTI improvementExpected low (+0.001 to +0.004) precisely because the sibling falsification is good evidence against transform-of-a-derivative arms. Ranked last for that reason.
implementation costLow — one gradient, one skeleton, one coherence gate.
data obtainability checkAlready materialised (same GeoDAWN release).

Not proposed, and why

  • Adding mass anywhere. Every live gain in this project has been a removal, and the addition arms measured 0.0022–0.0077 credit per dot against a τlive of 0.05416 — ten to twenty-five times too expensive.
  • Anything needing the dropped seismic bands. The supplied ieq_n100a15 has an autocorrelation of 0.9986 at 1 km: it carries no fault-scale information, and no transform can recover it.
  • Hyperspectral or InSAR. No free, official, footprint-covering source has been verified, and the competition's own feature stack already includes the relevant derivatives.
