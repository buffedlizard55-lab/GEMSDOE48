1 · highest relative potential
H48-A: co-located gravity–magnetic edge geometry		 Layers: isostatic gravity anomaly and slope; reduced-to-pole magnetics; TMI horizontal/vertical gradients; detrended-elevation slope as a nuisance-control layer.
Signature: compute oriented horizontal-gradient maxima; retain line segments where gravity and magnetic edges are co-located and strike-coherent, then downweight edges explained by topographic slope or survey seams.		 A buried fault/contact may juxtapose density and magnetization contrasts beneath alluvium without a surface scarp in USGS/INGENIOUS. Non-fault mimics include lithologic contacts, intrusions, and survey-processing seams; therefore agreement and seam controls are essential. Distinct from GEMSDOE47 H47-B’s single TMI_up150 scale-persistence detector (an owner-reported public-proxy trial that lost to a random control) and from generic ridge stacking: this hypothesis requires cross-physical-property edge geometry and a topographic nuisance test. Cost: medium.
Expected gain: highest qualitative upside in this list, not numerically forecast.
Sources: official competition training_features.tif lists gravity and magnetic layers but requires DrivenData login; USGS GeoDAWN lists public area TIF archives; USGS Nevada gravity Data Series 42 lists an accessible 4.2 MB data ZIP. Metadata/link presence was verified; direct USGS byte transfer failed TLS in this sandbox, and no matching layers are in this checkout.
Status: hypothesis only; blocked pending verified local rasters and spatial holdout.
2 · medium-high relative potential
H48-B: 3-D hypocenter planes with focal-mechanism consistency		 Layers: USGS ComCat event latitude/longitude/depth/time, focal mechanisms (strike/dip/rake), with provided strain invariants as optional context.
Signature: robust 3-D plane/line clustering of hypocenters, requiring consistent focal-mechanism nodal-plane orientations; project supported planes to the surface with uncertainty footprints.		 A buried active structure can host a plane of seismicity without a surface trace. Non-fault mimics include volcanic/hydrothermal swarms, quarry blasts, and poorly located events. This differs from the competition’s 2-D earthquake-density layer and the prior H47-GSA strain/thermal association: the feature is event-level 3-D geometry and mechanism coherence. It remains a hypothesis, not evidence that seismicity marks geothermal vents. Cost: medium-high.
Expected gain: medium-high qualitative upside; magnitude unknown.
Source check: official USGS FDSN ComCat API is public. For the approximate GeoDAWN bounding box and M≥2 since 2000, live count requests returned 16,919 events, 5,292 with focal-mechanism products, and 474 with moment-tensor products on 2026-10-06. The API is obtainable; the point-level response has not been ingested into this repo.
Status: source available; model and blocked validation not run.
3 · medium relative potential
H48-C: fault-related drainage deflection on lidar terrain		 Layers: competition 1 m DEM tile list or USGS 3DEP bare-earth DEMs.
Signature: extract channels from multi-scale flow accumulation; score abrupt lateral offsets, paired channel bends, and aligned knickpoints on alluvial fans, with stream-order and fan-surface controls.		 Young blind or subtle faults can deform drainage and fan surfaces where relief is too small or eroded for a continuous scarp. Non-fault mimics include lithologic changes, fan avulsion, landslides, roads, and incision. Prior H20/H25/H28/H47 methods focused on scarps, ridges, or persistence; this targets channel-network displacement geometry rather than a scarp line. Cost: high (terrain tiling, flow routing, edge harmonization).
Expected gain: medium qualitative upside.
Source check: USGS states 3DEP products are free and without use restrictions, and the competition lists 1 m DEM links. Exact 1 m coverage for every holdout block was not inventoried here.
Status: not viable for scoring until coverage and tile seams are audited.
4 · lower relative potential
H48-D: signed radiometric alteration asymmetry across candidate structures		 Layers: GeoDAWN potassium, equivalent uranium, equivalent thorium and K/eTh or K/eU ratios; survey block/flight-height metadata; detrended slope for confound control.
Signature: oriented cross-strike paired-flank ratio contrast and phase-consistent boundaries after within-survey normalization.		 Hydrothermal fluid-rock interaction may alter near-surface radiometric composition adjacent to permeable structures that are not mapped. Non-fault mimics include lithologic boundaries, soil moisture, vegetation/cover, and survey seams. GEMSDOE46 already explored scarp/radiometric concordance; this tests signed cross-fault asymmetry rather than simply requiring a radiometric/scarp overlap. Cost: medium.
Expected gain: low-medium qualitative upside because related radiometric fusion was already tried.
Source check: USGS GeoDAWN release lists radiometric products and TIF archives; the files are public by catalog listing, but direct bytes were not retrieved in this environment.
Status: exploratory; not validated.
