Method — combining two families while preserving their disagreement

Dempster-Shafer evidence theory (Dempster 1967; Shafer 1976), long established in GIS favorability mapping as an alternative to weights-of-evidence, applied to the two strongest independently-built fault surfaces this program has produced.

1 · Why not average the two surfaces?

The dotted family (spacing-tuned dot-thin of a detector field; best live 0.2778) and the tip/step-over family (fault-tip kinematic protection, Euler SI=0 corroboration, analog-field transfer; live 0.2632) were built by different pipelines with different physics. A weighted average of the two rasters would force every pixel into a single blended number and destroy exactly the information that matters for discovery: where the two approaches disagree. Under a naive mean, a dot present in only one family becomes 0.5 — numerically indistinguishable in spirit from weak agreement, and with no record of which family believed it.

Dempster-Shafer theory instead assigns each source a mass function over the frame Θ = {F, N} (fault / no-fault) with an explicit ignorance component, and combines them with Dempster's rule, which carries forward an unassigned mass wherever the sources do not force a decision and quarantines direct contradiction as conflict.

2 · Mass assignment (discounted, per pixel)

Each family surface si(x) ∈ {0,1} (both artifacts emit binary dots) becomes a discounted Bayesian mass function with source reliability αi = 0.99:

massformulameaning
mi({F})αi·sicommitted belief “fault here”
mi({N})αi·(1−si)committed belief “no fault” (the pipeline looked and rejected)
mi(Θ)1−αiuncommitted / ignorance

Committed negative evidence is deliberate: both pipelines are tuned detectors whose zeros are rejections, not missing data. (The alternative simple-support construction with no negative evidence produces zero conflict everywhere and no disagreement layer — it was considered and rejected for exactly that reason.)

3 · Dempster's rule of combination

For m1 = (f1, n1, u1), m2 = (f2, n2, u2), with conflict K = f1n2 + n1f2 :

quantityformulathis fusion, α=0.99
combined belief Bel(F) = m12(F) (f1f2 + f1u2 + u1f2)/(1−K) both families: 0.9999 · one family: α/(1+α) = 0.49749 · neither: 0
unassigned mass m12(Θ)u1u2/(1−K) both: 0.0001 · one: 0.00503 · neither: 1.0 — the diagnostic layer shipped as …ds-unassigned-mass….tif
conflict Kf1n2 + n1f2 0.9801 at every single-source dot — the disagreement map, shipped as …ds-conflict….tif
plausibility Pl(F)Bel(F) + m12(Θ) upper bound of warranted belief

The resulting tier structure is intrinsic to Dempster's rule: single-source evidence can never reach belief 0.5 under symmetric discounting (α/(1+α) < 0.5 for α < 1) — corroboration is required for conviction. The belief surface is normalized to [0,1] by dividing by its maximum (the intersection tier), giving tiers {1.0, 0.49754, 0}.

4 · From belief to submission: the decision rule

The competition metric is the distance-weighted Tversky index with α=0.2 (FP), β=0.8 (FN), triangular kernel R=300 m, and — critically — graded predictions p(x) enter the formula directly (no binarization). Marginal analysis: a dot of value v that is the best cover of a truth pixel improves DTI iff its geometric credit clears ≈ 0.2·DTI ≈ 0.055, independent of v; value scales upside and downside together. The only decision the metric forces is the support set — and how much confidence each tier should carry is then a risk question answered empirically by the holdout:
