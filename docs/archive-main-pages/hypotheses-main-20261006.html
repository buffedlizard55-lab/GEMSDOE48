Ranked candidates (expected DTI improvement vs implementation cost)

#1 · H49-2 — Paleo-discharge ascent nodes: sinter/tufa × dilational stepover jogs

Layers: GDR paleogeothermal features (spring deposits: sinter, tufa, travertine — 244 px already rasterized on-grid in the GEMSDOE30 mirror) × dilatation-rate band of the official feature stack × the fused family dot set. Signature: within 300 m of each paleo-deposit, relocate/seed dots at local maxima of dilatation rate and at stepover dilational jogs of nearby family traces (classic fluid-upflow loci; Faulds & Hinz 2015 stepover-relay geometry). Why off-catalogue: Quaternary spring deposits are direct sedimentary evidence of past geothermal upflow; many sit on structures too small, buried, or eroded to appear in QFaults/INGENIOUS. Differs from prior art: the paleo layer has been derived (GEMSDOE30 external receipt) but no shipped arm ever used it; prior “addition arms” used heat flow, Euler, LiDAR — never paleo-discharge. Cost: low (layers already mirrored; pure numpy). Expected gain: low-to-moderate on the Initial Round metric (small mass), but high for the Final Prize Round, where expert-verified new faults expand the label set — corroborated candidate upflow nodes are exactly what experts can confirm.

#2 · H49-1 — 2-m temperature probe advection anomalies along off-catalogue lineaments

Layers: GDR 2-m temperature probe surveys (3,439 probes in-grid; derived raster mirrored in GEMSDOE30) × detrended elevation × RTP magnetics. Signature: probe-temperature residuals after removing the elevation + latitude conductive trend; warm residual clusters (>+2σ) collinear with magnetics/DTI lineament segments; emit along the connecting segments. Why off-catalogue: probes sample actual shallow heat advection — blind, fluid-conducting faults leave a thermal footprint with no required surface expression, so they are structurally absent from a morphologically-compiled catalogue. Differs from prior art: H38-1 used conductive heat-flow residuals (DeAngelo et al. 2022); GEMSDOE36 used well geothermal gradients. Shallow advective probe residuals with lineament collinearity have never been built. Cost: moderate (trend fit + collinearity statistics). Expected gain: moderate-to-high (direct thermal evidence), coverage clustered near access roads — must be blocked-out in validation.

#3 · H49-3 — Hydrothermal alteration index (K/Th) at lineament intersections

Layers: GeoDAWN radiometrics (K%, Th% — available via the GDR/GeoDAWN release and mirrored in the sibling public layers) × two lineament fields (DTI dots & magnetic-gradient ridges). Signature: hydrothermal alteration depletes K relative to Th (argillic/sericitic zones); low K/Th ratio localized at lineament–lineament intersections = permeable conduit + altered wall rock. Why off-catalogue: alteration halos persist under alluvium where surface traces vanish — precisely the covered-basin regime where the catalogue is sparsest. Differs from prior art: GEMSDOE46 tried scarp × radiometric-total fusion; the K/Th ratio transform restricted to intersection nodes is new. Cost: moderate. Expected gain: moderate.

#4 · H49-4 — Magnetic damage-zone texture (rolling variance of RTP)

Layers: reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly (feature band 2) × Quaternary volcanics mask (GEMSDOE30 mirror). Signature: fault damage zones hydrothermally alter/destroy magnetic carriers → elevated local variance and entropy of RTP in 300–500 m bands along buried traces; skeletonize the variance field, NMS-thin to dots. Why off-catalogue: damage-zone width is detectable without any surface trace; the catalogue misses buried segments systematically. Differs from prior art: all prior magnetic families used gradients, Euler depths, or source-depth estimates — never texture statistics. Cost: moderate. Expected gain: moderate; risk: volcanic edifices produce similar texture — the volcanics mask is mandatory, which is why it ranks below #3.

#5 · H49-5 — Drainage-network fault offsets on the 1-m DEM

Layers: 1-m DEMs from the competition's own 1m_DEM_links.csv (official USGS 3DEP items). Signature: stream longitudinal-profile knickpoint index + offset/beheaded channel detection along range fronts (network-level transform, not local scarp kernels). Why off-catalogue: range-front faults are digitized incompletely; drainage anomalies expose unmapped strands and stepovers between digitized segments. Differs from prior art: prior LiDAR arms (7GEMSDOE, r7-nms3-dem10-scarp) detected local scarps only; network hydrography analysis has not been attempted. Cost: high (DEM volumes; the sandbox cannot fetch them — needs an unrestricted machine). Expected gain: moderate-to-high; ranked last purely on cost.

Validation-before-slot protocol for the top candidate

  1. Build the candidate layer from the mirrored data (already fetchable here via GitHub API).
  2. Blocked off-catalogue SGMC holdout: candidate emission vs same-dot-count random control and vs the shipped DS support (this page's arms), exact official metric.
  3. LOSFO-style far-field falsification (remove whole fault systems with a 600 m buffer): a candidate whose gain vanishes in the far field is catalogue-adjacency leakage, not discovery.
  4. Only a candidate passing ≥3/4 folds on both instruments may occupy a weekly slot.
