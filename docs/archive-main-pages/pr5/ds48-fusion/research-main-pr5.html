Interpreting the owner-reported 0.2778

The official metric, a conditional score inversion, the proxy limitation, and what the evidence cannot establish.

1 · The official metric, and the identity that makes emission a knapsack

From the competition problem description (page 967), with k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0) a triangular kernel in metres:

TPw = Σ_g max_x p(x)·k(d(x,g))          FPw = Σ_x p(x)·(1 − max_g k(d(x,g)))
FNw = Σ_g (1 − max_x p(x)·k(d(x,g)))    DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + ε)

This is the Tversky index with α = 0.2 and β = 0.8, and the organizers' own reference solution trains with TverskyLoss(alpha=0.2, beta=0.8) — an independent official corroboration of the weights. Two identities follow and are asserted in tests/test_metric.py:

FNw = |G| − TPw            (because p ≤ 1 and k ≤ 1)
S = Σx p(x),  M = Σx p(x)·max_g k(x,g),  FPw = S − M
DTI = TPw / (0.2·TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·|G|)

Marginal value. Under the stated metric, adding one unit of mass at realised kernel weight k raises the score iff k > 0.2·DTI. Using the owner-reported 0.2778 gives a nominal bar of 0.0556. Inverting two owner-reported anchors yields a model-derived 0.05416 only if the score pair, metric version, and truth population are comparable. Neither is an independent verification of the reported score or its associated TIFF.

Binary is optimal. For a pixel whose value is v, the score is (T₀ + v·k)/(D₀ + 0.2v) while it stays the argmax, whose derivative (k·D₀ − 0.2·T₀)/(D₀ + 0.2v)² has the sign of k − 0.2·DTI and does not depend on v. Every pixel is therefore pushed to 1 or to 0: a graded probability surface — including a normalised belief surface — strictly loses. That is why the submission emitted here uses values in {0.0, 1.0}.

2 · What the reported dotted-family sequence suggests

In the owner-reported examples, the pixel count falls 44,090 → 40,199 → 37,654 while the reported score rises 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778. This is consistent with improved average credit per emitted pixel after those particular removals, but the score/artifact mapping is not independently verified and the sequence alone does not establish causation or a general rule that removals will help.

The staff clarification (community thread) explains the mechanism: the scoring mask is pixel-exact and identical to the provided training labels, so the published catalogue is neither a target nor a shield. A dot near a known trace but far from a new fault is fully penalised, with no buffer. The measured null confirms it — one sibling artifact added all 60,988 catalogue pixels as predictions and scored exactly the same as its twin.

For four artifacts with owner-reported live scores, comparison against a spatially blocked, density-matched catalogue holdout (4 quadrants × 2 draws, common random numbers) gives:

Spearman ρ = -1.0. In this four-point owner-reported sample, the proxy ranks the artifacts in the exact reverse of the reported live order. That is sufficient to disqualify this catalogue proxy as a promotion gate here, not to characterize every possible proxy. This repository records the limitation rather than quoting its favourable catalogue-only number. Sibling repositories reached the same conclusion independently (IR-32-PROXY-01, IR-H19-HARNESS-LEAK), and GEMSDOE29's own receipt shows the pure SGMC arm at 0.0446 on that proxy but 0.0512 live, i.e. at chance.

5 · What the covering geometry actually says

The metric pays for the largest gap near each truth pixel, not for the number of dots. Measured nearest-neighbour spacing of the shipped emissions (exact Euclidean, in metres, via a k-d tree):

artifactmedianp75p95max share > 600 m
dotted_027083003617005,1247.0 %
tip_026323163616716,9976.7 %
dotted_b2_prune_027783003617215,1247.7 %

7 % of dots sit more than 600 m from their nearest neighbour, so the midpoint of that gap earns zero credit while both bounding dots still cost 0.2 each. The hexagonal lattice is the provably optimal covering of the plane (covering density 2π/(3√3) = 1.2092 versus π/2 = 1.5708 for the square lattice, a 23.0 % saving at equal covering radius), so a covering-optimal re-emission should have helped. Measured, it did not cleanly: at matched mass the best of the 11 spacings tried (5.6 px, 37,499 dots) is x1.019 on the only non-anti-monotone layer and x12.5 on the catalogue layer, whose ordering is known to be inverted. The reason a covering argument under-delivers here is that the shipped dots are information-weighted along the ridges rather than uniform over the corridor, so covering optimality alone does not establish the best re-emission.
