The owner-reported 0.2778 result, with its limits

The table records owner-reported scores from the project's history; they are not independently verified organizer receipts, and no local TIFF hash is tied to a leaderboard row here. A dated 2026-10-06 public leaderboard snapshot placed 0.2778 at rank 13, not at the top. The reported sequence is consistent with useful removals in that specific family, but it does not establish causation or generalize to a new detector.

The mechanism, arithmetically. With the official identity FNw = |G| − TPw, the metric is DTI = TPw / (0.2·TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·|G|). Deleting one unit of mass lowers the denominator by 0.2·(1 − k) and the numerator by the credit it was earning. So a dot is worth emitting iff its realised kernel weight exceeds 0.2·DTI — 0.0556 using the owner-reported 0.2778. The 0.05416 value is a model-derived break-even conditional on the reported anchor pair and metric assumptions, not a verified per-dot measurement. Full derivation: Interpreting reported 0.2778.

What the historical experiment built and measured

Dempster&ndash;Shafer fusion DERIVED

The dotted family and the tip / step-over family agree on 69.6 % of the grid and disagree on 14,724 pixels. They overlap substantially: 31,614 of b2’s 37,654 positive cells are also in the tip mask, so statistical independence/distinctness is not supported. Dempster’s rule is shown here as a research diagnostic, not as a calibrated fusion. On the frame {F, ¬F}, with Shafer reliability discounts a = 0.60. Belief is 0.8400 at agreement and 0.3750 where only one family supports a pixel.

Not the naive mean &mdash; measured MEASURED

Pearson r = 0.991085 and Spearman rho = 1.000000 on the union. Values differ (mean |delta| = 0.1423) but the ranking is identical, because a union pixel has belief 1 in its own family, so both statistics are monotone in the other family’s belief alone. Reproduced honestly rather than claimed as a win.
