What the reported sequence suggests—and what it cannot prove

The project history records an owner-reported sequence of 44,090 pixels at 0.2600, 40,199 at 0.2708, and 37,654 at 0.2778. This is consistent with the particular catalogue-flank removals increasing average credit per emitted pixel in that reported family. The mapping between reported scores, uploaded bytes, and the named local files has not been independently established, so the sequence does not prove causation or a general rule that removing pixels will help.

Under the published DTI identity, a prediction pixel with realized kernel credit k has positive marginal value when k > 0.2 × DTI. Substituting the owner-reported 0.2778 gives a nominal threshold of 0.05556. A separate 0.05416 threshold comes from a model inversion of two owner-reported anchors; it is conditional on comparable score/metric versions and truth populations, not a direct measurement of each pixel's value.

Conditional inversion is not observation of hidden truth

Inverting the two reported anchors under the stated metric assumptions yields an internally consistent estimate of roughly 12,632 hidden new-fault pixels. Those labels are not available in this repository, the score-to-file mapping is not independently verified, and the estimate depends on the official metric and artifact assumptions holding. Treat it as a model-derived scenario, not a known truth-set size, verified recall, or forecast.

Why this does not clear a new fusion

The catalogue-proximity proxy reverses the four known owner-reported live anchors (Spearman ρ = −1, n = 4), so it is not a promotion gate. The corrected SGMC proxy is public-derived and does not establish private-label performance. The Yager candidate loses to the dotted, tip, and naïve-mean baselines; the DS48 re-emission's owner-derived SGMC DTI is 0.09068 versus 0.09613 for dotted. Neither has an organizer score or observed portal acceptance.
