Five prospective geological hypotheses — none slot-cleared

Decision context. The requested H48 Yager fusion failed the corrected SGMC proxy gate. The prior H48-1 Dempster candidate also did not beat b2 on its reported spatial holdout; LSI estimates are not precise enough to claim a tie. The following are new, falsifiable data/transform hypotheses, not validated candidate submissions. Ranking is qualitative relative upside per implementation cost. No numeric ΔDTI forecast is justified. No weekly slot may be used until a candidate beats the corrected holdout best and clears the written raster-format gate.

Novelty here is operational and checkout-scoped, not a claim that no related concept has ever been tried. The owner’s full site inventory in the README records prior LiDAR scarp/ridge work (GEMSDOE7), combined topo-geophysical work (GEMSDOE16), and scarp–radiometric work (GEMSDOE46). N1, N2, and N5 therefore name specific transforms/controls that were not implemented in this checkout, not wholly unprecedented data families. The old H47-B TMI-up150 multi-scale persistence arm is not counted as new: the owner-reported public-catalogue screen says its pooled locked-block proxy DTI (0.02755) lost to its fixed-seed random control (0.03716). Those are not organizer/private scores or independent replication.

1 N1 — native-resolution 1 m LiDAR scarp detection USGS 3DEP bare-earth 1 m DEMs; paired profile curvature across a convex crest and concave toe, measured perpendicular to candidate strike at metre scale, then aggregate detections to the 100 m grid. Low-relief scarps can disappear at 100 m and may be absent from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. LiDAR scarp/ridge work already exists in GEMSDOE7; the checkout-scoped proposed difference is measuring paired profile-curvature signatures at native resolution before aggregation, then adding geomorphic controls. This is a refinement, not an unprecedented data family. Mimics: roads, terraces, channel banks, landslides, and fan edges. Highest qualitative upside; unknown numerically. High cost (tile inventory, seams, streaming, native-resolution processing). USGS says 3DEP products are free and without use restrictions; the competition data page lists 1 m DEM links but requires login. A sample TNM query returned tile metadata; full study-area coverage, source bytes/checksums, and block coverage were not verified here. No spatial holdout run.
2 N2 — cross-property gravity–magnetic edge geometry Competition isostatic-gravity anomaly/slope and magnetic anomaly/gradients; USGS GeoDAWN magnetic grids and Nevada gravity stations where aligned. Compute oriented horizontal-gradient maxima; retain edges co-located and strike-coherent across properties, suppressing slope and survey-seam artifacts. Buried fault/contact juxtaposition can create density and magnetization boundaries without a surface scarp. Combined topography/geophysics has precedent in GEMSDOE16; the checkout-scoped difference proposed here is co-located, strike-coherent gravity and magnetic edge geometry with explicit terrain nuisance control, rather than TMI-only scale persistence. Mimics: lithologic contacts, intrusions, flight-line seams, and gridding boundaries. Medium-high qualitative upside; unknown numerically. Medium cost. Competition stack is login-gated. USGS GeoDAWN lists public magnetic/radiometric TIF archives; USGS Nevada Gravity Data Series 42 lists a public data archive. Catalog metadata were checked, but direct binary transfer failed TLS here and alignment/coverage is unverified. No spatial holdout run.
3 N3 — 3-D hypocenter planes with focal-mechanism consistency USGS ComCat latitude/longitude/depth/time and focal mechanisms (strike/dip/rake), optionally strain invariants. Cluster in 3-D, fit planes, require mechanism consistency, then project supported planes to a surface trace with location uncertainty. A buried active structure can organize seismicity without a surface trace. This differs from the supplied smooth 2-D earthquake-density band and prior strain/thermal associations. Mimics: volcanic/hydrothermal swarms, quarry blasts, poor locations, and unrelated intersecting structures. Low–medium qualitative upside; event-location uncertainty relative to the 300 m scoring kernel is a major unmeasured limit. Medium-high cost. Official USGS FDSN count queries for the approximate study bbox since 2000, M≥2, returned 16,919 events, 5,292 with focal-mechanism products, 474 moment tensors (2026-10-06). Counts verify endpoint availability only; no event payload or detector is validated. No spatial holdout run.
4 N4 — fault-related drainage deflection on lidar terrain 1 m DEM-derived flow network, channel centerlines, stream order, fan-surface geometry; detect aligned lateral offsets, paired bends, and knickpoints rather than scarp lines. Subtle young deformation can offset channels or fans where a continuous scarp is absent. Distinct from prior scarp/ridge persistence because the target is drainage-network displacement. Mimics: fan avulsion, lithology, incision, roads, and landslides. Medium qualitative upside; unknown numerically. High cost (flow routing and tile/seam harmonization). Same public USGS 3DEP catalog and competition DEM links as N1. Per-block 1 m coverage was not inventoried; no terrain was downloaded or validated. No spatial holdout run.
5 N5 — signed radiometric asymmetry across candidate structures GeoDAWN potassium, equivalent uranium/thorium and K/eTh or K/eU ratios; normalize by survey block/flight-height metadata; test signed, opposite-flank contrasts after slope/cover controls. Hydrothermal alteration or fluid-rock interaction may create asymmetric near-surface radiometric patterns around permeable structures. Scarp–radiometric fusion/concordance already appears in GEMSDOE46; the checkout-scoped proposed difference is a signed, cross-strike asymmetry statistic rather than generic overlap. Treat it as an adjacent refinement, not a wholly untried radiometric family. Mimics: lithologic boundaries, soil moisture, vegetation/cover, and survey seams. Low–medium qualitative upside; unknown numerically. Medium cost. Official USGS GeoDAWN release lists radiometric products and TIF archives. File bytes, block coverage, licensing details for derivatives, and score impact are not verified here. No spatial holdout run.

Promotion and validation protocol

  1. Select one detector and freeze layers, processing, confounds, thresholds, spatial folds, and promotion rule before looking at held-out proxy labels.
  2. Obtain/check official data rights, checksums, CRS, footprint, tile seams, survey provenance, and compatibility with contest terms. A catalog entry or count is not a validated raster.
  3. Generate fold-specific predictions without using held-out labels; compare with the corrected tip, dotted, and naïve-mean anchors using src/gems48/validation.py and exact known-fault masking.
  4. Require an improvement over the best baseline and consistent blocked folds. Public SGMC proxy gains are necessary evidence only, not proof of private-score gain.
  5. Re-open the final GeoTIFF, verify its [0,1] range, and resolve the official null/NaN-outside vs portal range-check conflict before any upload. No candidate on this page is slot-cleared.

All availability and source caveats are in the source register and machine-readable source checks.
