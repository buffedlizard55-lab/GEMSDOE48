What the fusion does—and does not say

Each binary source value is treated as an operational opinion: m(F)=0.5p, m(not F)=0.5(1−p), m(Θ)=0.5. After normalized Dempster combination, source agreement at a positive pixel yields fault belief 0.75; a one-source-only pixel yields belief 1/3, residual ignorance 1/3, and raw pre-normalization conflict K=0.25. The disagreement remains visible instead of being deleted by a consensus rule.

Important: the inputs are sparse binary emissions, not calibrated probabilities; interpreting zero as counter-evidence is an assumption. The `rho=0.5` discount is preregistered, not estimated. The two surfaces share 31,614 positive cells (Jaccard 0.6599), so they are not treated as independent.

Arithmetic-average check: the output is not the arithmetic mean: 47,905 in-footprint cells differ; in the positive union the mean absolute difference is 0.221661 and maximum absolute difference is 0.25. Exact comparison matters because both surfaces are highly correlated and sparse.
