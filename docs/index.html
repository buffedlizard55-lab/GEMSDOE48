The Novel Fusion: Pignistic × Conflict-Price

Method: Dempster's rule with metric-geometry kernel-credit belief surfaces. Output is the pignistic probability BetP(F) = Bel(F) + m(Θ)/2 weighted by (1−K) where K is the raw conflict.
Why it's unique: Unlike raw Bel(F) averaging, this explicitly suppresses where the families disagree — the disagreement enters the surface itself.

Why Dempster–Shafer with Pignistic Conflict-Pricing?

The brief asks us to "combine your two best-performing families with a rule that preserves disagreement instead of averaging it away." A naive weighted average of the two surfaces would wash out exactly the information in where they disagree.

Dempster's rule of combination (Dempster 1967; Shafer 1976, A Mathematical Theory of Evidence) combines two evidence sources by:

  • Carrying forward mass of unassigned belief m(Θ) wherever the sources are uncertain
  • Computing raw conjunctive conflict K where sources actively contradict each other
  • Normalizing to redistribute conflict mass (the canonical Dempster rule)

The pignistic transform (Smets 1990) converts the resulting belief function into a probability for decision-making: BetP(F) = Bel(F) + m(Θ)/2. This is the unique coherent betting probability.

Conflict-pricing multiplies by (1−K), explicitly suppressing the surface where the two families disagree. This is the key novelty: the disagreement enters the SUBMISSION surface itself, not just a separate diagnostic.
