# Why 0.2778 is the family's best live score, and what is achievable — 2026-10-07

This note answers the brief's two questions with the metric's algebra and the three live
scores of the dotted family, not with intuition. Every number is reproduced by
`scripts/live_ladder_analysis.py` → `evidence/live_ladder_20261007.json`.

## 1. The metric is a weighted Tversky index with a 300 m kernel

With α = 0.2, β = 0.8 and kernel `k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0)` (organizer page 967), a binary
submission `P` scored against truth `G` gives

    DTI = T / (T + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN) = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·FP + 0.8·|G|),

where `T` is the kernel-weighted overlap, `FP = |P| − T` and `FN = |G| − T`. Two consequences
drive everything else:

* **The denominator is dominated by 0.8·|G|** (the hidden truth mass), which the submitter
  cannot change. The only levers are `T` (recovered truth) and `FP`.
* **Marginal value of one more predicted pixel.** Adding a pixel with expected kernel weight
  `w` raises the score iff `w > 0.2·DTI` (≈ 0.056 at 0.2778). Removing a pixel pays iff its
  weight is below the same bar. A single emitted pixel therefore needs only a ≈ 6 % chance of
  being a full hit to be worth keeping — which is why *sparse, well-spaced* coverage beats
  dense lines, and why "confidence" grading cannot help a binary-equivalent map.

## 2. The ladder A → B → C explains 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 exactly

| Rung | Dots | Live | Removed | Removed dots' location |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A | 44,090 | 0.2600 | — | Poisson-disk dots, d = 2.83 px over the H19-5 backbone |
| B | 40,199 | 0.2708 | 3,891 | all ≤ 100 m from the public catalogue |
| C | 37,654 | 0.2778 | 2,545 | all 100–200 m from the catalogue |

If `n` removed dots carried no credit, `1/s_before − 1/s_after = 0.2·n/T`. Solving each step
independently gives `T/π = 5,073` (A→B) and `5,470` (B→C) — the same number within 7.5 %.
That consistency is the evidence for the mechanism: **the organizer masks pixels on known
USGS/INGENIOUS faults from scoring** (community post 11516), so dots sitting on or next to
catalogue traces are pure 0.2-weight false positives. Pruning them is a free score gain,
and C is simply the rung at which the family stopped pruning. Nothing geological was added
between A and C; the gain is bookkeeping against the metric.

Taking the mean `T/π ≈ 5,270` and solving the 0.2778 equation for `|G|`: hidden-truth
kernel mass `|G|/π ≈ 14,300`, so C recovers about **37 %** of it, and the average C dot
carries 0.14 of a full hit.

## 3. Why the *dotted* construction beats lines and fusions

* A line of 100 m pixels along a candidate trace spends ~3 pixels per 300 m of strike; a
  Poisson-disk dot pattern at 283 m spacing spends one. Because the kernel radius is 300 m,
  one dot already collects most of the credit a line would; the other two pixels are mostly
  0.2-weight FPs. The dotted family therefore has ~3× the hit-rate per emitted pixel of the
  dense backbone it was cut from (dense H19-5, 129 k px, scored 0.1922 — same `T`, triple the FP).
* H33-D (tip/step-over, 0.2632) protects fault tips and adds Euler clusters; its union with C
  shares 31,614 of its 41,865 pixels (Jaccard 0.66). Two generators this correlated carry
  almost no independent evidence, which is why every fusion tested in this repository
  (Dempster ρ = 0.5, Yager, arithmetic mean, graded belief) lost to the plain union on the
  blocked proxy — fusion can only re-weight the same 48 k pixels and the metric ignores weights
  once a pixel is emitted (`docs/research/holdout-results-20261006.md`).

## 4. Is > 0.2778 achievable? Is > 0.3195 or > 0.3774?

At constant mass (37,654 dots), the score is a function of recovered truth alone:

| Target | Needed `T/π` | Needed recall of hidden mass | Relative to C |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.2778 (C) | 5,272 | 0.37 | — |
| 0.2888 (#8) | 5,480 | 0.38 | +4 % |
| 0.3195 (#7, DARD) | 6,063 | 0.42 | +15 % |
| 0.3262 (#3) | 6,190 | 0.43 | +17 % |
| 0.3345 (#2) | 6,348 | 0.44 | +20 % |
| 0.3774 (#1) | 7,162 | 0.50 | +36 % |

(At fixed mass the denominator `0.2·|P| + 0.8·|G|` ≈ 18,977 is constant, so the score is linear
in `T`; figures as in `evidence/live_ladder_20261007.json`, ±10 % with the inversion's assumptions.)

So **> 0.2778 is achievable with modest new information**: every additional 300 dots with a
≥ 20 % hit-rate adds ≈ +0.002. **> 0.3195 requires a different information source**, not a
different combination of the same geophysical layers — the measured per-dot re-rankers at
100 m top out at 1.4× (U/K) and the circular 2.3× (SGMC distance), far short of the ~2.6×
separation that would let a removal pay for itself. **0.3774 requires recovering ~57 % of
the hidden mass**, i.e. finding roughly half again as many test faults as the backbone sees.
The leaderboard leader's margin is consistent with a method that *sees the scarps* (lidar)
rather than one that re-weights 100 m geophysics; the H50-1 pilot (2.3–3.2× catalogue lift
at 3 m inside smooth terrain, versus ≤ 1× for the 2 m u8 descriptors at 100 m) is the first
measurement in this repository pointing the same way.

## 5. What this implies for a weekly slot

The organizer's Phase-2 rule (community post 11527/7: the final truth is expanded by expert
review of *all* Phase-1 submissions) means a submission's value is partly what it teaches the
experts. A map that adds genuinely new, lidar-visible scarp candidates has option value even
when its Phase-1 gain is small — but only if it does not dilute the 37,654 dots that already
carry 0.14 credit each. That is why the H50 candidate is constructed as **C ∪ additions**,
never as a re-weighting or pruning of C.
