# Why the owner-reported 0.2778 may have scored well — and what is not supportable

**Status:** cautious, evidence-based explanation; not a score certificate, hidden-label estimate, or prediction for a new file. The exact local B2 bytes are marked `UNSCORED` by their producer and are not linked to a leaderboard row by an organizer receipt. This note supersedes the former live-ladder/hidden-truth inversion as a score explanation. The raw historical receipts remain for audit, but the derived bounds below must not be used.

## Short answer

The reported 0.2778 may have benefited from the official metric's **300 m triangular partial-credit kernel**, its **α=0.2 / β=0.8** distance-weighted Tversky asymmetry, and a **pruned / spacing-tuned** dotted surface that removes low-credit predictions—especially predictions near a known-catalogue mask—while preserving higher-credit corridor locations. That mechanism is plausible and consistent with the owner-reported A/B/C ladder. It is **not proven**: there is no organizer receipt tying the local B2 raster's exact bytes to 0.2778, no private expert-label data, and no causal ablation on the organizer's test labels.

A score above 0.2778 is possible in principle because the same one-time leaderboard observation contained higher participant-level values. But **no score above 0.2778, and especially not 0.3195, is supportable as a prediction for H56B or any local file**. The former 0.0649 live-equivalent estimate and H55 “ceiling” are invalid because they rely on an incorrect metric identity; the corrected derivation and executable counterexample are in [`metric-identity-erratum-20261007.md`](metric-identity-erratum-20261007.md).

## Official metric facts that support the plausible mechanism

The [official competition page](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) defines distance-weighted Tversky scoring. For distance `d`, a prediction receives a triangular kernel credit `k(d)=max(1-d/300 m,0)`: full credit at a truth pixel, decreasing partial credit with distance, and no credit beyond 300 m. The official weights are α=0.2 for false positives and β=0.8 for false negatives. Thus a predicted corridor dot can receive partial true-positive credit near an unmapped fault, while predictions with little truth-near support add false-positive cost. This makes spatial placement and selection/pruning relevant; a count of dots alone does not explain the score.

The organizer has separately stated that known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue faults are masked from scoring ([forum topic](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516)). The precise treatment of near-catalogue locations, phase-specific truth, and exact score attribution remains bounded by what that statement and local receipts establish; it does not authenticate any local TIFF.

## Owner-reported ladder: consistent with pruning, not a causal test

The repository's ladder is owner-reported, not organizer-receipted:

| Label | Dots | Reported DTI | Interpretation limit |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A | 44,090 | 0.2600 | Owner-reported family rung; exact file-to-score link not available here |
| B | 40,199 | 0.2708 | Owner-reported pruning rung; exact file-to-score link not available here |
| C / local B2 mirror | 37,654 | 0.2778 | Local producer page calls exact mirrored bytes `UNSCORED` and gives a separate 0.2747 projection |

Each rung removes or repacks dots. A plausible explanation is that the removed subset carried relatively little private-label kernel credit while still contributing false-positive cost; pruning could then raise DTI. The B2 producer's local note says that its B=2 prune retained 37,654 dots with none within 200 m of the catalogue, and labels the exact bytes `UNSCORED`. This supports a **hypothesis about the owner-reported family trajectory**, not an organizer-verified explanation of 0.2778.

The ledger's former `scripts/live_ladder_analysis.py` output fitted hidden-truth mass and per-dot credit by treating `FPw=S−TPw`. That is generally false: the official formula has `FPw=S−Q`, where `Q=Σ_x p(x) max_g k(d(x,g))` is prediction-centred, while `TPw=Σ_g max_x p(x)k(d(x,g))` is truth-centred. Therefore old inferred `T`, recall, “break-even” values, recovered-truth totals, and reachable/unreachable conclusions are **not supported**. They remain in [`evidence/live_ladder_20261007.json`](../../evidence/live_ladder_20261007.json) as historical calculations only; they are not reinterpreted as ground truth.

## Leaderboard context, used sparingly

A single dated leaderboard observation recorded the top displayed score as 0.3774, 0.3195 at rank 7, and a 0.2778 row at rank 13. It did not identify any local file hash. Older repository notes disagree on displayed submission counts, so that discrepancy is left unresolved rather than reconciled with another read. This is a participant-level, date-specific observation, not a permanent table or score certificate. The [DrivenData Terms of Use](https://www.drivendata.org/termsofuse/) restrict automatic monitoring/copying and also manual monitoring/copying without prior written consent; the repository does not poll the board or republish its row-by-row table.

## Evidence about the current H56B-NF ablation is separate from the 0.2778 row

The current downloadable file is H56B-NF, a post-hoc no-catalogue-flank Dempster–Shafer mass-assignment ablation on the dotted B2 and H33-D tip/step-over owner mirrors. It is byte/pixel-distinct within the checked repository, but is not a new geological detector or preregistered candidate; it has no organizer score. Under the matched four-fold public-proxy holdout, higher DTI is better:

| Public-map proxy | H56B-NF mean | H49 mean | Difference | Folds beating H49 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Catalogue | 0.056305 | 0.095353 | −0.039048 | 0/4 |
| SGMC >300 m off catalogue | 0.068987 | 0.100751 | −0.031764 | 0/4 |

These public maps are not private expert labels; candidate surfaces were not reconstructed independently inside every fold. The result does not prove the organizer score will be low, but it fails the comparable blocked gate and is a reason **not to spend a weekly submission slot**. The old H56B score projection of 0.0649 is invalid and must not be substituted for an actual score or as a “conservative” forecast.

The earlier with-flank H56B rebuild has unique bytes, but is within `1.8e-7` of the prior H56 surface (top-37,654 Jaccard 1.0), so it is a precision/encoding derivative rather than a meaningfully new candidate. H56B-NF is a distinct ablation, not evidence that its geological concept or score is novel.

## Decision

- **OK to download:** yes, H56B-NF for inspection; its local format/recomputation audits pass. NaN outside follows the documented null convention but can trigger an all-pixel portal range check (`portal_range_error_immune=false`), and organizer acceptance is untested.
- **OK / recommended to submit:** no. H56B-NF loses to H49 on every public-proxy fold; there is no private-label evidence or organizer acceptance receipt.
- **Can H56B-NF be claimed to beat 0.2778 or reach 0.3195?** No. The evidence does not support a numerical prediction above either value. Possibility is not supportability.
