H49 is a Yager budget variant; V1–V3 sweeps used h32-1/h33d tips).

Construction, per pixel, on the fixed 100 m EPSG:32611 grid:

1. **Belief surfaces in the metric's geometry.**
   `b_i(x) = max over committed pixels y of k(d(x,y))`, `k(d)=max(1−d/300 m,0)`
   (the competition kernel). Belief therefore decays smoothly away from each
   family's committed dots instead of asserting hard "no fault" 100 m from a
   dot — the semantic defect of fusing raw sparse emissions.
2. **Mass functions (two-sided simple support, Shafer §11.2 discounting).**
   `m_i(F)=a_i·b_i`, `m_i(notF)=a_i·(1−b_i)`, `m_i(Θ)=1−a_i` with
   `a_dotted = RHO_MAX = 0.95`, `a_H36 = 0.95·(0.2710/0.2778) = 0.926746` (the legacy code calls this `a_tip`, but H36 is not a tip/step-over source). The reported-score ratio was a heuristic reliability-weight choice, not verified local-file performance, truth calibration, or a probability.
   The 0.95 ceiling is preregistered ("no source is perfectly reliable"); a
   first build with `a_dotted=1.0` was withdrawn because it forces
   `m12(Θ)=u1·u2/(1−K)≡0`, killing the required unassigned-mass diagnostic
   (withdrawn v1 sha `2c01d212…b0db`, receipt preserved as
   `evidence/build_ds50_v1_anchor100_withdrawn_receipt_20261007.json`).
3. **Canonical normalized Dempster rule.** Bel(F), m12(Θ) and raw conflict
   `K=m1(F)m2(notF)+m1(notF)m2(F)` are stored; the submission surface is
   Bel(F) divided by its in-footprint max, i.e. normalized to [0,1].

Provable properties (tests/test_ds50.py): masses sum to 1 everywhere; the rule
is commutative; at full agreement Bel=1, K=0, m(Θ)=(1−a1)(1−a2); at full
opposition K=a1·a2 and the unassigned mass is strictly positive (the
disagreement is carried, not averaged away); Bel≡1 on parent A's pixels only
when a1=1.

## What the proxy comparison does—and does not—show

The table above reports the official metric evaluated against the listed public-proxy masks under the recorded blocked-fold protocol. In those tests H50 loses to both parents and to the union on both proxies, while beating the naive mean and the earlier raw-sparse fusion recipe in the stated comparisons. These are conditional public-proxy observations; they do not establish private-label performance, a leaderboard score, a causal geological explanation, or a future score ceiling.

For notation, let `S` be emitted mass, `T` truth-centred weighted true-positive credit, `Q` prediction-centred matched credit, and `N` truth mass. The metric is

```text
TPw = T
FPw = S - Q
FNw = N - T
DTI = T / (0.2*T + 0.2*(S-Q) + 0.8*(N-T) + epsilon)
```

`T` and `Q` use different maxima and are generally unequal. The earlier one-variable derivative argument did not establish that binary `{0,1}` values are globally optimal for arbitrary prediction rasters. Nor does a measured public-proxy advantage for a union prove that union is optimal on the private target. H50's thresholded/binary row is an empirical proxy comparison only.

The old narrative also inferred a per-dot bar of about `0.052`, asserted that the reported `0.2778` file sat at that bar, and estimated the mean credit required to approach leaderboard rows. Those quantities depend on the invalid `FPw=S-TPw` inversion and unverified local-file attribution; they are withdrawn, not score estimates or thresholds. D-S belief is intersection-like in this construction and can preserve uncertainty where parents differ, but these properties alone do not demonstrate better prediction. The separate `m(Theta)` and raw `K` layers describe mass assignment/conflict diagnostics, not calibrated truth probability. The current result is simply that the recorded H50 surface loses to the better parent and union on both public proxies; no weekly slot is cleared.
