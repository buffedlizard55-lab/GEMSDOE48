**Construction (`src/gemsdoe48/ds58.py`, `scripts/build_h58_ds_b2_h32.py`).** Each parent's committed dots become a kernel-credit field `b(x) = max_y k(d(x,y))`, with `k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0)`. Each source gets a two-sided simple-support mass with discount `a` (`RHO_MAX = 0.95` for dotted B2; `0.95 × 0.2649/0.2778 ≈ 0.906` for tip H32-1, an owner-score ratio, not a calibration). The masses are combined with the normalized Dempster rule, and `K` and `m(Θ)` are kept as separate layers. The output is `m12(F)` divided by its maximum over the emission support and clipped to [0,1]. **Emission is restricted to the union of committed dots**, so the 300 m halos are not submitted.

**Why not the H50-style surface.** H50 evaluates the same rule on the whole footprint. That spreads about 288,000 units of mass over 769,098 cells. The metric charges each unit of off-target mass at 0.2, so the spread scores **0.07659** against 0.09539 for B2 (`evidence/holdout_h58_20261008.json`). The dot-supported restriction is what makes this a usable artifact.

## 3. Why 0.2778 scored well (mechanism, with the limits)

**Metric.** `DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw)`. The organizers' clarification (2026-09-21; [forum thread](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516)) says pixel-exact known-fault cells are masked. A prediction near a known trace but far from new-fault truth is fully penalized. So a dot 100–200 m from a known trace costs about one unit of FP unless a new-fault pixel lies within 300 m.

**Verified geometry** (`evidence/h58_parent_geometry_20261008.json`, script `scripts/audit_h58_parent_geometry.py`):

| Parent | Dots | Min distance to catalogue | Within 200 m |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| B2 (owner 0.2778) | 37,654 | 224 m | **0** |
| dotted base (owner 0.2708) | 40,199 | 141 m | 2,545 |
| tip H33-D (owner 0.2632) | 41,865 | 100 m | 3,894 |
| tip H32-1 (owner 0.2649) | 42,294 | 100 m | 4,637 |

B2 equals the 0.2708 base minus exactly the 2,545 dots at 141–200 m (none added), which confirms the owner's audit. No dot in any parent lies on a catalogue pixel.

**Proxy test of the mechanism.** Proxy truth = SGMC off-catalogue (62,122 px), official metric, full footprint, catalogue pixels zeroed. Random subsamples (seeds 1–3):

| Truth density | B2 − base 0.2708 | B2 wins (seeds) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 62,122 px (full proxy) | **+0.00009** | 3/3 |
| 25,000 px | **+0.00085** | 3/3 |
| 12,632 px | **+0.00124** | 3/3 |

(`evidence/holdout_h58_density_20261008.json`.) **Direction reproduced: pruning the 100–200 m flank helps, and it helps more as truth gets sparser. Magnitude not reproduced:** the owner's ladder gap is +0.0070, about 5× the largest proxy effect. The proxy's density is an assumption, and no locally verified hidden density exists (the withdrawn 12,632 value appears only as a sensitivity point). IR-H58-10.

**Flank rule on the other family.** Applying the same rule to tip H32-1 (dropping its 4,637 dots within 200 m, leaving 37,657) moves it from 0.09476 to **0.09534** on the full footprint. That recovers about **92%** of the gap to B2 (0.09539). At 25,000 and 12,632 px the gap is under 0.0001 (`evidence/h58_flank_transfer_explore_20261008.json`, exploratory). **On this proxy, most of the owner-reported 0.2649-vs-0.2778 difference is explained by the flank rule, not by family identity.** This is a proxy finding and unverified against private labels.

| union of flank-pruned B2 and tip (exploratory) | 0.09559 | 0.08114 | 0.06350 | 4/4 but below B2 at both sparse densities |

Three points follow:

1. **Parent differences at the 0.26–0.28 level are about 0.001 or less on this proxy.** The proxy cannot rank these candidates on the leaderboard's scale.
2. **Rankings depend on truth density.** H49 leads at 62k and 25k px but is below B2 at 12.6k px. Under a minimax-regret rule over these three candidates, H49 has the smaller worst-case regret (0.00041 vs 0.00613 for B2). That is an observation about the decision rule, not a promotion. H49 is also not cleared.
3. **H58-A is the weakest of the B2-based candidates** at the sparse densities (−0.0009 to −0.0013 vs B2). Its extra 5,420 tip dots within 200 m of the catalogue are the flank dots the B2 rule removes.

The most promising lead is the union of flank-pruned B2 and tip (4/4 quadrants, +0.0002 full footprint, −0.0003 at 12.6k). It is exploratory: it was found after the gate was written, and its support duplicates H48-1. It needs a new preregistration before any scoring that could promote it.

## 7. Pre-registered gravity-edge test (H58-G1): NOT CLEARED

Preregistered in `docs/research/h58-gravity-edge-preregistration-20261008.md` before scoring: isostatic-gravity horizontal-gradient maxima (Blakely & Simpson 1986, [USGS page](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/approximating-edges-source-bodies-magnetic-or-gravity-anomalies), DOI [10.1190/1.1442197](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1442197)) from the official feature band 13 (`iso_grav_anom`) in the owner-mirrored stack, filtered to >300 m from the catalogue, budget 37,654.

* **Budget infeasible.** Only **12,600** eligible maxima exist (13,743 maxima in the footprint). The emission is therefore 12,600 cells, not 37,654. IR-H58-09.
* **Full footprint:** G1 **0.02777** vs B2 0.09539 and random-37,654 0.06913. **Quadrant wins vs B2: 0/4.** Density 25k: G1 0.02637 vs B2 0.08119. Density 12.6k: G1 0.02361 vs B2 0.06380. **Gate fails on every criterion.**
* **Exploratory, post-gate, mass-matched at 12,600 cells:** precision within 300 m of truth **0.0869** for G1, vs **0.0833** for random and **0.0836** for the footprint base rate. So the gravity-edge signal is at chance on this proxy. This is an exploratory note, not part of the decision.

Receipt: `evidence/h58_gravity_edge_holdout_20261008.json` · script `scripts/validate_h58_gravity_edges.py`.
