7 · Why 0.2778 worked, and whether we can beat it

With the published metric, and writing T for weighted true positives, F for wasted mass and K for hidden truth pixels:

1/DTI = α + α·(F/T) + β·(K/T)        α = 0.2, β = 0.8

Adding one unit of emitted mass raises the denominator by exactly α = 0.2 wherever it lands. A cell therefore pays for itself only if its expected credit exceeds α·DTI ≈ 0.056 at DTI 0.278 — i.e. it must sit within roughly 284 m of a hidden fault pixel. Every recorded live move in this campaign that gained score did it by deleting mass whose credit was below that bar (121k → 60k → 44k → 40k → 37.6k cells: 0.1922 → 0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778). That is a credit-density result, not a probability-calibration result, and it is why H33-2-B2 leads the family.

Can we beat 0.2778? Honestly: not with these two families alone. This session measured every reachable direction and found the dotted parent sits at a local optimum:

  • Additions. The tip family's extra cells return 0.0357 proxy credit per cell as a raw union but 0.0599 when the fused field is used to place them — 68 % better allocation at identical mass. Against the calibrated live bar the modeled point estimate is −0.003 with a bracket of −0.010 to +0.005; the sign is unresolved and does not justify a submission slot.
  • Removals. No tested subgroup of the dotted parent is free: every quintile of every geometric feature carries 0.13–0.17 proxy credit per cell, 3–4× the removal bar.
  • Hypothesis H-A (lineament coherence pruning) is falsified — see hypotheses.

The single largest remaining lever is knowledge, not method: to move from 0.278 toward the 0.3774 leading public score requires finding faults the two families never saw, which needs either the 419 MB official feature stack or new external geothermal/geophysical data. Both are blocked by this sandbox's network egress; the exact free official URLs and their obtainability are listed on the hypotheses page.
